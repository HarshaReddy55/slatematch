01Component Guide - DashboardExpand guide
What it is

The home screen: the headline results, a summary strip (the ribbon) worked out from the latest run, and the history of every run.

How it is calculated

Touchless rate = payments posted on their own, divided by all payments in the run (hard payments are counted, not hidden). Unapplied cash = the total of leftover amounts across the run's matches. Latency = the average solver time stored on each match.

What it means

If the unapplied-cash figure grows from run to run, money is arriving faster than the agent can explain it. That points to poor remittance data, not a weak solver.

OPERATE — 04 / RUN RECONCILIATION

Run Reconciliation

Five agents execute in sequence on the active case; the sixth, Briefing, composes its summary on demand. The flagship match is already open below - its cash conservation derivation and the why-not counterfactual proof on screen, nothing buried behind a click.
JD LINKThe role asks for custom algorithms for matching and reconciliation. This page is that engine, running live on the active case.
Step 1 - narrow the list: for each payment, the engine first narrows the unpaid invoices using the evidence it has, in this order: invoice references in the remittance, then the purchase order, then OCR-repaired references, then an exact amount on the payer's own ledger, then a fuzzy payer-name match, and only then that customer's whole open ledger. The solver never searches beyond the paying customer's ledger. Step 2 - solve: the MILP solver picks the set of invoices whose total, after discounts and any accepted deduction, equals the payment exactly (the cash conservation equation). Among exact sets it prefers older invoices, and it treats any unexplained leftover (residual) as very costly - a penalty of 1,000,000 - so it would rather propose nothing than hide a difference. Open any match to see its invoices and a why-not check for an invoice it did not pick. An invoice used by one payment cannot be used again in the same batch, and sending the same batch twice replays the first result instead of posting twice.

RUNComponent Guide - Batch reconciliationExpand guide
What it is

One full pass of the real engine over a set of payments: narrow the candidates, solve the matches, judge deductions, and raise exceptions for the rest.

How it is calculated

For each payment: the candidate list narrows by reference, then PO, then OCR repair, then amount-plus-payer, then fuzzy payer name, then the customer's whole ledger. The solver then finds the invoice set that equals the payment exactly, preferring older invoices. The confidence rule and the auto-post rule are on the Methodology page, and every match shows its own worked calculation (its derivation).

What it means

AUTO_POSTED rows are finished work. PROPOSED rows are the agent asking a person to approve. Exceptions are the agent refusing to guess - each one says what to do next.

What you can do

Open any match to read its worked calculation and to test why another invoice was not picked. To change behaviour, edit the rules in Policy Studio and run again.

Held for sign-off - the maker-checker queue

Maker-checker sign-off queue

Full width, on the page - approve individually, or post every solved held match in one action (an unmatched wire in the queue is decided on its own).

Matches the agent solved but would not post alone - because policy held them (for example the maker-checker threshold) or a person must sign - plus any wire the agent could not match at all, held here so a person decides its fate. Approving a match posts it; approving an unmatched wire parks it in suspense and issues no document. Rejecting records the decision and takes the item out of the queue. Every decision is written to the audit trail.

INSIGHT & GOVERNANCE — 07 / FORECAST AND COLLECTIONS

Forecast and Collections

When unpaid invoices are likely to turn into cash, and which customers to chase first. The forecast comes from the trained payment-delay model; the collections queue is a score combining amount, days overdue and predicted delay. The model's accuracy is shown on the Agent Health page.
JD LINKThe role asks for predictive forecasting, work prioritisation and anomaly identification. This page is all three, driven by the trained models and the money at risk.
This page and the Ledger are the same data, seen two ways. The Ledger lists every unpaid invoice. This page takes the invoices still open after the latest reconciliation - part-paid ones at their remaining balance - predicts when each one will be paid, and stacks them into weeks of expected cash - click any week to see the invoices inside it. For each invoice, the model predicts how many days the customer will take to pay, based on that customer's past behaviour, the amount, and the time of year. Sampling many possible payment dates around those predictions gives the weekly P10 / P50 / P90 range - a range a treasurer can plan against, instead of one made-up number. Cash expected after the 8-week horizon is not dropped: it sits in the Beyond row. The collections queue scores each invoice by money at risk: amount, days overdue, and predicted delay combined.

EVALS & ARCHITECTURE — 10 / EVALUATIONS

Evaluations

Three lenses, all computed live. The active case graded against the answer it stated up front; the solver benchmarked against a simple baseline on labelled cases where the correct answer is known; and the ten scenario packs - full pipeline cases, each with its expectations stated before the run.
JD LINKThe role asks for applied data science with results that can be measured. This page measures the solver against answers that were fixed before the run. The delay and validity models are graded on the Agent Health page.
Each test case has a correct answer marked by a human: the invoices the payment truly settles. Both solvers get the same shortlist a real payment would get. Accuracy counts how often the solver's choice equals the marked answer; the exact rate counts answers that leave $0.00 unexplained. The greedy baseline is fast and sounds reasonable, but it fails when the right choice depends on age and deductions together - that is why real payments use the MILP solver. The scenario packs run the whole pipeline (resolution, solver, deduction judgement, policy) on ten stated cases, and every check must pass. The delay and validity models are graded separately, on the Agent Health page, against a held-out test set.

Request path of one payment

Step Stage What happens
1Ingestion + transformCSV, PDF, image or API input is normalised to the canonical schema: synonym header mapping, source fingerprinting, row quarantine with reasons, OCR on image remittances. Remittance text goes to the parser (Gemini when configured, a deterministic parser otherwise, with the mode shown) and every extracted reference is verified against the open ledger.
2Candidate resolutionThe ledger narrows down an evidence ladder: exact reference, purchase order, OCR-repaired reference, amount-plus-payer, fuzzy payer name, then the customer's whole ledger. Duplicate wires are fingerprinted (payer, amount, currency) and stopped before matching, and candidates are hard-filtered to the payment's own currency.
3MILP solver (HiGHS via scipy)Subset selection under the cash conservation equality with the age-weighted, penalty-priced objective from the Methodology page. A Rust subset-sum kernel runs inside the solve path: it decides a payment only when its exact set is provably unique (bounded at 18 candidates), and every set the MILP returns is re-verified in exact integer cents before it is believed. Kernel and Python path are equivalence-tested on 203 shared cases.
4Deduction intelligenceClaims are classified to the ERP reason taxonomy, z-scored against the payer's own judged claim history in the workspace, and decided by the expected-value dispute rule. Unearned discounts are claimed back as their own reason code.
5Posting + exceptionsExact, confident matches auto-post: their invoices settle and their SAP clearing document is issued in the same moment. Everything else becomes a proposed match (which issues its document on approval) or a tracked exception with counterfactuals. Approving an unmatched wire parks it in suspense with no document at all. All of it lands in the audit log with the run's policy snapshot.
6Insight layerDelay and validity models (scikit-learn, time-ordered holdout) work from the post-reconciliation residual ledger only - settled and part-paid money is never double-counted - and feed the forecast bands (400-simulation Monte Carlo, P10/P50/P90) and the collections queue; the copilot answers only from these stored records.

Design rules that hold the system together

1. AI proposes, math decides. No LLM output can post cash; it can only propose fields that deterministic components verify.
2. Money is integer cents. No floats anywhere near a posting; residuals are exact, never rounding artefacts.
3. Every decision carries its derivation. If a number cannot be recomputed from records the product shows, it is not shown.
4. Exceptions are a workflow, not a failure. The tail is where analysts earn their keep, so it is designed first-class: counterfactuals, recommended actions, audit trail.
5. Policy is configuration. Thresholds that change behaviour live in tenant policy, versioned with each run - never buried in code.

Scaling the solver

Question Answer
Why doesn't the MILP explode?The solver never sees the ledger. Candidate resolution prunes each payment to a payer-scoped shortlist first (references, PO, OCR-repaired references, amount-plus-payer, fuzzy payer, whole ledger), so the NP-hard core runs on a handful of invoices per payment, not thousands.
Raw vs prunedA 20-invoice pool has 2^20 (about a million) raw subsets; resolution prunes each payment to a short, payer-scoped shortlist first, and HiGHS branch-and-bound closes the rest. The enumeration and kernel fast paths are bounded at 18 candidates, which keeps the worst case small and provable.
Latency evidenceSolver time is stored on every match; the Dashboard ribbon averages it for the latest run. The live run streams each payment's solve time as it happens.
EquivalenceThe Rust kernel and the Python solver are proven identical on 203 shared cases; neither is trusted on its own say-so.

Product thesis

01/
The problem

Cash application is a conservation problem with an information problem on top: every cent received must land somewhere auditable, but the evidence arrives as messy remittance text, short pays and silent deductions. Incumbent platforms advertise 85 to 99 percent auto-match, yet implementations run for months and the exception tail still eats the analyst day. The tail is the product opportunity.

02/
The design rule

AI proposes, math decides. Probabilistic components (LLM parsing, ML delay and validity models) extract and score; deterministic components (the MILP solver under a cash conservation equation, the policy engine, the expected-value dispute rule) make every posting decision. Anything unreconciled becomes a tracked exception with counterfactuals - never a silent write-off.

03/
The wedge

A pilot called the replay: send us your last 90 days of payments and open invoices as CSV. SlateMatch replays them and shows, cent by cent, what would have auto-posted, what would have been disputed, and what your team touched that it should not have had to. Proof before platform.

04/
North-star metric

Touchless cash rate at $0 residual. Supporting: match accuracy on labelled evaluations, residual dollars, exception ageing, dispute recovery value, solver latency.

Roadmap, honestly labelled. Live ERP write-back (SAP, Oracle, NetSuite), bank-file ingestion (BAI2, MT940, lockbox formats), persistent multi-tenant storage, and SOC 2 are the known table-stakes gaps for enterprise sale. This build covers the decision core those integrations feed: matching, deductions, forecast, collections, evaluation and audit.

Honesty labels. All data in this workspace is synthetic and labelled as such. Market figures quoted anywhere in this product are vendor claims used as context, never this product's results. Win probabilities in deduction intelligence are transparent heuristics, and the UI says so where they appear.
